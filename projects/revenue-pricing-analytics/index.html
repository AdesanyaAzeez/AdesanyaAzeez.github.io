CASE STUDY · PAYMENTS PLATFORM

Revenue &
Pricing Analytics

A grain-preserving, transaction-level revenue model combining effective-dated pricing, processor costs, and billing events into auditable, analytics-ready datasets for finance and operations teams.

• SQL • Data Modelling • Airbyte • Metabase
Role Analytics Engineering Lead, Revenue domain
Area Data modelling · Pricing · Reconciliation

Context

On a payments platform processing across multiple regions, fee plans, and payment rails, revenue sat at the intersection of several related but disconnected datasets: transaction events, product and plan catalogs, merchant-level pricing configurations and processor settlement.

Finance, business, and operations teams each needed their own cut of the revenue numbers — but those numbers were being produced differently depending on who ran them, with no single auditable transaction-level record that tied a billing event through to the pricing and cost lines that determined the final revenue amount.

Problem

Revenue calculations lacked a consistent, centralized, transaction-level foundation, creating several connected challenges:

  • Manual computation — revenue calculations relied on cross-spreadsheet computations across multiple transaction and pricing dates, making it difficult to consistently apply pricing and trace individual results.
  • No centralized pricing repository — pricing configurations were not maintained in a single structured source, making it difficult to consistently apply the correct pricing across transactions.
  • Broken effective-date handling — pricing changes and amendments were not reliably tied to their effective periods, creating a risk of applying incorrect pricing to transactions.
  • No transaction-level breakdown — revenue could not be consistently traced from the individual transaction through the applicable pricing, fees, costs, and final revenue amount.

Approach

The design goal was a grain-preserving, transaction-level revenue fact — every billing event on the platform is enriched, once, with all the pricing and cost context needed to answer any downstream revenue question without re-joining raw systems.

  1. Inventory & unify inputs. Identify and structure the required pricing, transaction, merchant, product, processor-cost, and billing inputs. Establish consistent fields and effective-date information so the different sources can be used together.
  2. Establish the pricing model. Build structured pricing entities and relationships covering merchant-specific pricing, product/plan configurations, processor costs, and effective periods. The model supports both specific pricing configurations and appropriate fallback rules.
  3. Build the transaction-level revenue model. Enrich each transaction with the pricing and cost attributes required to calculate the applicable fees, costs, and revenue at transaction level. The resulting model provides a single analytical record from which downstream revenue reporting can be derived.
  4. Validate and reconcile. Add checks around pricing coverage, missing cost information, effective-date matching, and calculated outputs so exceptions can be identified before reaching downstream reporting.
  5. Publish analytics-ready outputs. Publish the transaction-level model and aggregated reporting outputs across key business dimensions for BI, finance, and operational analysis.

Sanitized Conceptual Architecture

This is not a medallion / bronze-silver-gold warehouse architecture. The pipeline is intentionally short and deterministic:

  • Sheets are the computed source of truth for products & pricing. Plans, tiers, effective windows, and fee rules are modelled and version-controlled by the revenue team directly in Sheets before ingestion.
  • Airbyte runs a scheduled sync that brings Sheets-configured product and pricing rows (alongside billing events and processor cost feeds) into ClickHouse staging tables with no intermediate transformations.
  • The revenue model is a single SQL query (effective-dated SCD2 joins, gross-fee and cost calculations) written once and deployed as a refreshable materialized view inside ClickHouse.
  • The MV publishes to a governed target table on a fixed schedule — wide, additive, per-transaction grain, with all metric columns pre-computed and identical across every downstream use case.
  • BI consumes only the published target table. Dashboards, reconciliation reports, and ad-hoc analyst queries all read from the same BI-ready surface — guaranteeing one definition of every revenue metric.

Key Engineering Challenges

  • Effective-dated pricing. Pricing configurations could change over time, meaning a transaction needed to be matched to the pricing that was valid at the time it occurred rather than simply using the latest configuration.
  • Pricing resolution. Multiple pricing levels and fallback rules needed to be resolved consistently so that the correct configuration was selected for each transaction. I structured the precedence explicitly within the model so resolution was deterministic and auditable.
  • Transaction-level traceability. Revenue needed to remain explainable at the individual transaction level, with the applicable pricing, fees, costs, and calculated revenue visible rather than only producing aggregated totals. Every row in the final table retained both its upstream identifiers and the matched configuration identifiers.
  • Data consistency and reconciliation. Pricing, transaction, and processor-cost inputs came from different sources and required validation before being used in revenue calculations. I added checks for missing mappings, incomplete pricing coverage, and unexpected calculation results so issues surfaced before downstream reporting used the output.
  • Decimal and financial calculation precision. Financial calculations required consistent numeric types and precision across transaction amounts, percentages, fees, and calculated revenue to avoid rounding and type-conversion issues. Precision and rounding rules were applied explicitly at the correct stage in the model rather than relying on implicit behaviour.

Outcome & Impact

Consistent revenue foundation

A single transaction-level revenue record — one source of truth used identically by BI, finance, and operations.

Effective-dated & auditable

Pricing changes, plan amendments, and cost rates are applied consistently to the correct transaction window.

Transaction-level explainability

Any revenue amount can be traced from an aggregate figure back to the individual transaction, matched pricing, and applicable cost lines.

Coverage & quality visibility

Missing pricing coverage, cost-population gaps, and calculation exceptions are surfaced via explicit validation checks during the build.

The pricing coverage checks also surfaced an under-billed pattern across a segment of configurations, enabling a structured remediation that aligned pricing behaviour with the documented product and plan terms — while keeping the revenue model itself deterministic and auditable through the correction cycle.
