ANALYTICS PLATFORM

Analytics
Data Warehouse

Centralising related data from multiple operational databases into a shared ClickHouse analytical environment, preserving useful source structure while adding derived analytical tables where required — making cross-system analysis simpler and analytical querying faster.

• ClickHouse • SQL • Data Modelling • Airbyte • Python
Role Analytics Engineer · Architecture & Build Lead
Area Warehouse design · ELT · Analytical Models · Data Governance

Context

Data for the business was distributed across multiple operational databases, with related entities and business processes spanning different systems. Analytical queries often required navigating these separate sources and joining data across database boundaries was difficult, which made reporting slower and more complex than necessary.

The goal was to establish ClickHouse as a central analytical warehouse — bringing relevant data from the operational systems into one analytical environment, improving query performance, simplifying cross-system analysis, and providing a reliable foundation for BI and downstream data models.

Problem

Analytical workloads were constrained by data being distributed across multiple operational databases:

  • Fragmented data — related business data was distributed across multiple databases, requiring cross-system lookups and joins to answer broader analytical questions.
  • Slow analytical queries — operational databases were designed primarily for transactional workloads, making complex analytical queries increasingly expensive as data volumes grew.
  • Repeated cross-database work — analysts and reporting workflows repeatedly had to retrieve and combine data from separate systems instead of working from a centralized analytical store.
  • Limited analytical structures — source tables were useful for operational workloads but did not always provide the derived or joined structures needed for efficient reporting and analysis.

Approach

Source tables were already partially clean and structured, so the warehouse did not require a heavy transformation pipeline. Instead, the approach focused on making related data usable together in one analytical environment:

  1. Centralize source data. Bring relevant tables from the different operational databases into ClickHouse, maintaining their source relationships and identifiers so cross-system analysis could happen within one analytical environment.
  2. Preserve useful source structures. Where source tables were already sufficiently structured for analytical use, retain their core structure rather than introducing unnecessary transformation layers.
  3. Build analytical structures where needed. Create additional derived and analytics-oriented tables for use cases where direct source-table querying was inefficient or where additional joins, calculations, or business logic were required.
  4. Optimize for analytical workloads. Design ClickHouse tables and query patterns around analytical access rather than operational transaction processing, with appropriate ordering, partitioning, and materialization where applicable.
  5. Provide a central consumption layer. Make the centralized ClickHouse environment the primary analytical source for BI and reporting, reducing the need for analysts to repeatedly query and combine multiple operational databases.

Sanitized Conceptual Architecture

The warehouse centralizes data from multiple operational systems in ClickHouse, preserving useful source structures while introducing derived analytical tables where required. This allows cross-system analysis and reporting to happen within a single analytical environment rather than repeatedly querying and joining separate operational databases.

  • Ingestion. Airbyte was used to replicate source tables into ClickHouse using the ingestion strategy best suited to each table's structure: CDC, XMIN, or Incremental sync. The determining factors were primarily the availability of a consistent primary key and a reliable cursor column, allowing each source to be replicated efficiently while maintaining incremental progress.
  • Source-aligned tables. Many source tables were brought into ClickHouse with their existing useful structure maintained rather than passed through unnecessary transformation layers. This retained enough of the original shape for traceability and made the warehouse understandable to anyone already familiar with the source systems.
  • Analytical tables. Additional derived and analytics-oriented tables were created for workloads that required more efficient joins, calculations, repeated aggregations, or business logic that would otherwise have to be rebuilt in every consumer query. The split was pragmatic: build a derived table existed only when it measurably improved a repeated analytical workload.
  • ClickHouse architecture. I designed the ClickHouse tables for analytical workloads and configured ReplicatedReplacingMergeTree across the cluster to maintain replicated table data across nodes. Sorting keys and table structures were aligned with common analytical access patterns to support efficient filtering, joins, and aggregations.
  • Consumption. BI tools and analysts query the centralized ClickHouse environment directly, rather than repeatedly pulling and stitching data from separate operational databases.

Key Engineering Challenges

  • Cross-database relationships. Related entities existed across separate operational databases, requiring consistent identifiers and careful mapping when bringing the data together in ClickHouse. I made identifier conventions and explicit mapping tables where needed so cross-system joins behaved predictably.
  • Analytical query performance. Queries that were manageable in individual operational systems became expensive when analytical workloads required large joins and aggregations. ClickHouse table design and query patterns were therefore optimized around analytical access.
  • Centralizing heterogeneous sources. Different operational databases had different structures and conventions. The warehouse needed to bring them into a common analytical environment without unnecessarily transforming already-useful source data.
  • Balancing source fidelity with analytical usability. The design had to preserve enough of the original source structure for traceability while introducing derived analytical structures where they provided better performance or usability.
  • Scaling analytical workloads. As the volume of data and complexity of queries increased, the warehouse needed to support larger analytical workloads without pushing that load back onto the operational systems.

Outcome & Impact

Centralized analytical access

Data from multiple operational databases became available within a single ClickHouse analytical environment.

Faster analytical workloads

Analytical queries ran against a warehouse designed for large-scale reads and aggregations rather than transactional databases.

Simpler cross-system analysis

Related data could be joined and analysed within ClickHouse instead of repeatedly retrieving data from separate operational systems.

Reduced operational DB dependency

BI and analytical workloads could be moved away from direct querying of the source operational databases.

The combination of source-aligned tables plus targeted derived analytical models also reduced the onboarding cost for new analytical work: analysts and reporting authors no longer needed to know which operational system held which piece of the puzzle, and could instead work start from a single ClickHouse environment with clearly named analytical tables already prepared for the most common reporting access patterns.
